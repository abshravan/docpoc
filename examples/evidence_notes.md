# Evidence notes (SYNTHETIC, educational)

These notes are a plain-language paraphrase written for this demo's evidence
lookup. They are NOT a copy of any publication and NOT a clinical reference.
The underlying criteria come from the Society of Radiologists in Ultrasound
(SRU) 2013 multispecialty consensus (Doubilet et al., NEJM 2013;369:1443).
Verify everything against the primary source before any real use.

## Crown-rump length and cardiac activity

The SRU 2013 consensus set the diagnostic crown-rump length (CRL) threshold for
calling early pregnancy loss, in the absence of cardiac activity, at 7 mm or
greater. A CRL of 7 mm or more with no detectable heartbeat is considered
diagnostic of pregnancy failure. The cutoff was deliberately raised to protect
specificity: earlier practice used smaller CRL values, which occasionally
produced false-positive diagnoses in pregnancies that were in fact viable. When
the CRL is below 7 mm and there is no heartbeat, the finding is suspicious for,
but not diagnostic of, loss, and a follow-up scan is recommended.

## Mean sac diameter without an embryo

A mean sac diameter (MSD) of 25 mm or greater with no embryo is diagnostic of
pregnancy failure under SRU 2013. An MSD between 16 mm and 24 mm with no embryo
is suspicious rather than diagnostic. As with CRL, the 25 mm cutoff is a
conservative, specificity-protecting choice; smaller MSD-based cutoffs used
historically were associated with false-positive loss diagnoses.

## Serial (interval) findings

When an earlier scan showed a gestational sac without a yolk sac, the absence of
an embryo with a heartbeat at 14 days or more later is diagnostic of failure.
When the earlier scan showed a sac with a yolk sac, the corresponding diagnostic
interval is 11 days or more. Shorter intervals (about 7-13 days for a sac
without a yolk sac, and 7-10 days for a sac with a yolk sac) are suspicious but
not diagnostic.

## Why specificity is prioritized

For early pregnancy loss, a false-positive diagnosis can lead to intervention
that ends a viable pregnancy. The criteria are therefore tuned for very high
specificity rather than maximal sensitivity: it is considered safer to call an
uncertain case "suspicious" and re-image than to over-diagnose loss. This is the
rationale behind the deterministic, fixed-threshold approach and the separate
"suspicious" tier that recommends follow-up only.

## Other suspicious findings

Additional findings that are suspicious for (not diagnostic of) loss include an
enlarged yolk sac (greater than 7 mm), an empty amnion (an amnion seen next to
the yolk sac with no embryo), a small gestational sac relative to the embryo
(less than 5 mm difference between MSD and CRL), and the absence of an embryo by
roughly six weeks after the last menstrual period.
